# Product page UX decision

Generated: 2026-09-21
Scope: DN Tech public product catalog and product detail template.

## TL;DR

The current product pages are structurally sound but visually generic: the catalog is a plain grid, and the detail template starts as a long information stack with the conversion path competing against content. The recommended change is a product-led layout: outcome-oriented catalog cards, a high-contrast detail hero with a static proof panel, visible page sections, and one repeated CTA. The tutorial/demo embed is removed from the detail template.

## Local findings

- `/products` fetches CMS products and renders category pills plus generic cards with three feature teasers and price.
- `/products/[slug]` already has the right content sources: features, use cases, pricing tiers, integrations, comparison table, testimonials, roadmap, FAQ, related posts, and CTAs.
- The detail template renders `BookDemoSection`, which can render a Calendly embed from `product.demoUrl`; this is the interactive block removed by the redesign.
- The backend product API already returns the detail payload. No new endpoint or database migration is necessary.
- Product seed data for dnPeople is outcome-oriented and already provides three feature pillars: Branch Operations Control, Payroll Confidence, and People Evidence.

## Design decision

### Product catalog

- Add a dark editorial intro with a single message: products are proof of how DN Tech works.
- Keep category filters, but present each product card as a decision card: category/status, product name, tagline, description, three outcomes, price signal, and “Lihat detail”.
- Preserve a custom-product CTA below the catalog.

### Product detail

- Replace the plain first viewport with a dark hero containing breadcrumb, category, status, name, tagline, description, primary/secondary CTA, and a static “product proof” panel.
- Add a compact facts row for status, audience/fit, and next step.
- Add anchor links to Features, Use cases, Pricing, FAQ; keep content in document flow rather than primary horizontal tabs.
- Keep existing data-driven sections and SEO JSON-LD.
- Remove `BookDemoSection` and the Calendly embed from the product detail template. The existing CTA still links to the product’s demo/contact route.
- Do not remove video testimonials elsewhere in the site; that is a separate content pattern and not part of the product detail request.

## Adversarial review

- Video can help explain complex software, so removal is not a universal conversion claim. The implementation keeps CTA/contact access and a proof panel; a future A/B test can reintroduce a video only where analytics or interviews show a need.
- A long page can become overwhelming. Anchor links, strong section headings, compact cards, and a repeated CTA reduce scanning cost without hiding core information.
- CMS products may lack imagery. The proof panel therefore does not require `heroImage` or `screenshotUrls`.
- Pricing and feature content remain CMS-driven; the UI change does not invent product claims.

## Validation plan

- Baseline and compare: CTA click-through, contact/demo starts, scroll depth to pricing/FAQ, and bounce rate by product slug/device.
- Run a 5-user moderated usability test: ask participants to identify fit, status, next step, and pricing without explaining the page.
- Target: ≥80% complete the “find fit + next step” task; no critical accessibility failures; LCP ≤2.0s p75 on mobile 4G for the first viewport.

## Source-backed rationale

Baymard’s product-page research covers descriptions, layout, plan matrices, FAQs, and cross-navigation, supporting a structured long page rather than a sparse detail page. Its findability research warns that important content hidden in horizontal tabs is often missed. web.dev supports keeping top-of-page media responsive and lightweight because resource selection affects LCP.
