# F4 — Adversarial review

## Challenge 1: Is “product engineering studio” too unfamiliar?

Likely yes for broad Indonesian buyers. The public competitor scan overwhelmingly uses “software house” or “software development” as the category entry. Treat “studio” as a method layer and explain it in plain Indonesian.

## Challenge 2: Does “software house” automatically mean low quality?

No. NIS Group and Technosindo use the term while describing discovery, product thinking, scope, integration, maintenance, and operational outcomes. The real risk is breadth and sameness, not the category label itself.

## Challenge 3: Can DN Tech claim product engineering today?

Partly. The repository shows first-party products, service/product separation, discovery, sprint delivery, testing, support, and SEO language. The weaker area is external case-study density and independently verified outcome proof. The public promise should stay bounded by what can be shown.

## Challenge 4: Could a hybrid label become confusing?

Yes, if both labels compete at the same hierarchy level. Use one category sentence, one method sentence, and three job-based routes. Do not put “software house / product engineering studio / digital transformation partner” in the same headline.

## Disconfirming evidence to collect next

- Last 20 qualified leads: what phrase did they use unprompted?
- Win/loss notes: did category familiarity or differentiation affect shortlist?
- Search Console: queries containing “software house”, “software development”, “MVP”, “integrasi”, and “aplikasi custom”.
- Five buyer interviews: ask respondents to restate what DN Tech does after viewing each variant for five seconds.
