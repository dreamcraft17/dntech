# DN Tech — Mekari-inspired design decision

*Generated: 2026-09-27 · Mode: deep research + market segment check*

## Decision

Use Mekari's solution-led SaaS architecture as a reference pattern, not as a visual clone. DN Tech should become a smaller, more credible “operational software studio + product ecosystem”: visitors choose a business job, see the relevant product/service, review evidence and boundaries, then take one contextual next step.

## What to implement

### 1. Navigation and page families

- `Layanan`: custom build, integrations, modernization, and delivery.
- `Produk`: dnPeople and future first-party products.
- `Bukti`: case studies, portfolio, testimonials, and delivery artifacts.
- `Resources`: blog, FAQ, guides, and quiz.
- `Tentang` and `Kontak` remain trust and conversion destinations.
- Keep existing URLs; change grouping and labels before changing routes.

### 2. Homepage sequence

1. Outcome/problem hero with preserved hero image.
2. “Pilih jalur” by job: bangun produk, rapikan workflow, pakai produk DN Tech.
3. Product/service modules with clear status and audience.
4. Proof strip: real case, process artifact, team/founder involvement.
5. Workflow/process and delivery boundaries.
6. Pricing signal or starting point where maintained.
7. FAQ and one contextual final CTA.

### 3. Product and service templates

Every detail page should expose:

- who it is for / not for;
- the operational problem;
- three to five workflows or outcomes;
- real proof or an honest readiness/status label;
- package/price boundary;
- integration or delivery scope;
- FAQ and next step.

### 4. Visual direction

- Keep DN Tech navy/teal/orange tokens; no Mekari logo, copy, imagery, or exact component cloning.
- Use a restrained blue-white SaaS canvas with strong type hierarchy, compact rectangular cards, visible rules, and product screenshots where available.
- Use dark navy only for hero/proof/CTA anchors; use white surfaces for dense operational content.
- Keep cards useful and scannable; avoid gradients, glassmorphism, floating blobs, decorative 3D, and unsupported metrics.

## Segment implication

The existing B2B segment scoring produces these priorities: Startup/product engineering (75.7, TARGET), multi-branch HR/payroll (74.2, TARGET), legacy integration (68.3, TARGET), and simple operational apps for UMKM (66.9, TARGET). Generic “developer murah” demand is WATCH (62.0) because differentiation is weak. The design should therefore lead with jobs and operational proof, not a generic agency grid.

## Adversarial review

- Mekari has a large ecosystem and brand proof; DN Tech must not imply equivalent scale. Label product maturity and show only maintained proof.
- More categories can create choice overload. The home entry stays at three jobs; the catalog holds detail.
- “Integrated ecosystem” is only credible when an actual integration exists. Use “workflow yang saling terhubung” for services until product integrations are real.
- A visual resemblance can become derivative. DN Tech’s differentiation is founder-led delivery, transparent scope, Indonesian operational context, and first-party products.

## Success measures

- User can identify the right job and next step in under 30 seconds.
- At least 4 of 5 moderated users can distinguish product vs service vs resource.
- Track clicks by job, product detail, pricing, case study, and qualified contact submission.
- No loss of existing public route coverage, SEO schema, or hero asset.
