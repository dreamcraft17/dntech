# DN Tech Product Catalog Layout Decision

*Generated: 2026-10-05 | Genre: implementation decision | Method: deep research + UX research + market-research + product/engineering review*

## Decision

Replace the generic three-column product grid on `/products` with a product-studio catalog:

1. category filters remain visible;
2. the CMS-featured product becomes a dark editorial hero;
3. the hero includes product identity, status, price signal, description, CTA, and a static workflow-proof panel;
4. remaining products appear in a numbered decision index with category, tagline, description, status, pricing signal, and detail CTA.

## Why this pattern

Public product studios and engineering-led software firms repeatedly use a hierarchy of featured proof, product/case context, supporting work, and process. The pattern is visible in i10Studios, thoughtbot, Looplogic, Orion Software, and DARN (sources 03–07). The goal is to make the catalog feel like a credible body of shipped product work, not a feature shelf.

## Product/UX requirements

### User task

“Find the product closest to my work, understand whether it is available, and open the next step.”

### Design requirements

- One primary visual anchor, not equal-weight cards.
- Do not invent customer metrics, logos, testimonials, or performance claims.
- Use the existing `Product` data contract.
- Preserve category filter behavior and locale-aware links.
- Keep all products discoverable below the featured block.
- Maintain keyboard focus rings and reduced-motion behavior.
- Keep copy outcome-oriented and compatible with HR/operations/product-engineering segments.

## Scope decision (PM)

### In scope

- Catalog hierarchy and card layout.
- Product proof panel with static UI abstraction.
- New localized chrome strings.
- Responsive behavior and focus states.

### Out of scope

- Product detail page redesign.
- New screenshots or media upload workflow.
- New customer metrics or proof claims.
- API/CMS data model changes.
- New JavaScript scroll animation library.

## Adversarial conclusion

The strongest risk is overclaiming proof. The static preview is intentionally abstract and labelled; actual product screenshots can replace it later only when assets are available and their background/alt treatment is reviewed. The second risk is hierarchy being mistaken for product ranking; CMS `featured` remains the source of truth and all supporting products remain visible.
