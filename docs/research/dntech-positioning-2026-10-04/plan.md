# DN Tech Positioning Research Plan

Generated: 2026-10-04  
Question: Should DN Tech keep **product engineering studio** as its primary positioning, or reposition as a **software house**?

## Decision to support

Choose a category label and message architecture that maximize qualified demand without flattening DN Tech into a generic custom-development vendor.

## Reframe

The decision is not only a naming choice. It is a trade-off between:

- **Category legibility:** will an Indonesian buyer immediately understand what DN Tech sells?
- **Differentiation:** does the label create a reason to prefer DN Tech over many software houses?
- **Proof fit:** can DN Tech credibly demonstrate the promise with current products, process, and case evidence?
- **Demand capture:** can the label capture existing search and referral intent without attracting mostly price-shopping leads?

## Falsifiable hypotheses

1. **H1 — Product engineering is more differentiating:** it should score higher on strategic distinctiveness and fit for startup/product-team buyers than “software house”.
2. **H2 — Software house is more legible:** it should capture broader Indonesian service intent and reduce explanation cost, but also increase generic/price-led competition.
3. **H3 — A hybrid architecture beats either label alone:** use “software house” as the category/search descriptor and “product engineering studio” as the method/differentiator.
4. **H4 — DN Tech’s strongest near-term wedge is problem-led:** MVP/product engineering, operational workflow software, and integration/modernization are more defensible than “we build any software”.

## Scope and exclusions

Included: DN Tech’s current product/service proof, Indonesian public competitor language, product-engineering category definitions, segment scoring, and positioning implications.

Excluded: paid keyword volume, conversion-rate claims, exact TAM/SAM/SOM, and customer-interview conclusions. Those require Search Console/ads data or primary interviews.

## Source strategy

- Primary/internal: DN Tech repository, current copy, existing market/content audits, existing segment JSON.
- Category/industry: IBM, Thoughtworks, thoughtbot.
- Indonesian competitive language: Embrace ID, NIS Group, Technosindo, plus public SERP scan for additional software-house pages.
- Method: Kotler-style five-criterion segment scoring via the local `segmentation_scorer.py` using the existing hypotheses. Scores are planning inputs, not observed market facts.

## Opposition queries

- “Product engineering studio” may be too unfamiliar for Indonesian buyers.
- “Software house” may be necessary as the only category phrase buyers recognize.
- First-party products could make “studio” credible, but current public case-study proof may be too thin.
- A hybrid label may become wordy or indecisive if the hierarchy is not explicit.

## Risk register

| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Internal scores confirm existing belief | Segment inputs were authored internally | Treat them as hypotheses; run lead/win-loss review and interviews |
| SERP scan is not keyword volume | Search-result prevalence is qualitative | Do not quote demand size; validate with GSC/Ads |
| Competitor pages make all categories overlap | “Software house” and “product” are often blended | Compare promise and proof, not labels alone |
| “Studio” overpromises product strategy | Product engineering implies discovery and iteration | Only claim services DN Tech can show: scope, product proof, delivery, support |
| Broad software-house traffic is low quality | Generic intent can bias toward price | Use the category term in metadata, but lead with named jobs and qualification |

## Stop criteria

Do not change the primary positioning solely from this pack if:

- qualified leads overwhelmingly use a different buying phrase;
- DN Tech cannot publish at least three concrete proof artifacts for the product-engineering promise;
- “software house” produces materially higher qualified-conversation rate after a controlled test;
- the hybrid message cannot be understood in a five-second homepage test.
